The Residents Hub — a Guide for Committee Members

A short, plain-English summary of what the Hub is, what it relies on, and what the committee should know. October 2026.

What the Hub is

The Bridgeman Downs Residents Hub, at bdrv.au, is the village’s own website. It was started in February 2026 to bring village information together in one place that every resident can reach from a phone, tablet or computer.

It is built and looked after by a volunteer, the RC Chair, and it costs very little to run: the website is hosted free by GitHub, and the information behind it is kept in free Google accounts. The regular costs are the yearly renewal of the bdrv.au name and the small per-search charge for the Price Checker’s AI searches.

What it does for residents

  • What’s on: events, registration for Social Club events, the Village Calendar, Fireside Talks, City Hall Concerts, and photo and video galleries.
  • News and notices: the monthly Bridgeman Times, RC Notices, announcements, The Roundup of news.
  • Community: Classifieds, Life in Our Community, Travel Pics, a Price Checker, village history and Down Memory Lane.
  • Information and help: transport, useful links, contacts and emergency numbers, help with phones and technology, the village map.
  • Residents Only (with the Residents PIN): meeting minutes, financial reports, bank statements, budgets, the Contact List, and a private way to raise a concern.
  • Transparency: “What We’re Working On” shows the progress of every issue raised with Village Management.

What it does for the committees

  • Members Area: each committee member chooses their name and sees the tools for their role.
  • Action Registers for the RC and the SC, and the Issues Tracker shared with the Community Manager and Manor Advisor.
  • Social Club finances: recording transactions, bank and cash balances, live dashboard and reports — with dedicated tiles for the SC Treasurer.
  • Asset Register of items bought with residents’ funds.
  • Documents: uploading minutes as drafts and marking them approved; managing events, registrations, announcements and the Village Calendar.
  • Text Updates to opted-in residents, sent through the Mobile Message text service (working since October 2026).

Privacy and PINs

Since October 2026, private areas use personal PINs. Each committee member and resident sets up their own PIN with a one-off welcome code from the Hub administrator, and can change it at any time. The PIN is checked by the Hub’s PIN Manager rather than written into the page, and the administrator decides in the PIN Manager which areas each person can open. If someone leaves a role, the administrator removes that role in the PIN Manager; nobody else’s PIN needs to change. A forgotten PIN is reset by the administrator.

Two things still need care. First, during the changeover the old shared PINs keep working until 30 November or 31 December 2026, depending on the page, and they can be found in the page code until they are removed. Second, the Residents Contact List and the Residents Only documents still read their information from published Google Sheets, so a determined person could reach that information without a PIN; the View Transactions page also still has no PIN. Strengthening those pages is recommended (see the Technical Guide). Sign-in details for the Hub’s accounts are kept only in the private Hub Secrets Register, never on the website.

The main risks, and what is recommended

  1. Only one person knows how it all works. This handbook is the first step. A second trusted person should be given access to the accounts and shown how to make a routine change.
  2. Some parts depended on a personal account. From October 2026: everything now belongs to the Hub Administrator account, bdrv.hubadmin.
  3. Some private information could still be reached by a determined person. Personal PINs now protect most areas. Next: retire the old shared PINs when the changeover ends, move the Contact List and Residents Only documents off published Sheets, and add a PIN to View Transactions.
  4. The domain must not lapse. Record the renewal date and set it to renew automatically.

If something goes wrong

  • A page looks wrong or a change hasn’t appeared: wait a few minutes and reopen the page — most delays clear themselves.
  • Information seems to have gone missing: it is almost always still in the Google Sheet behind the page. Nothing is lost by a page failing to display it.
  • The whole site is down: check the bdrv.au domain hasn’t expired, then check GitHub.
  • Every change is recorded, so any page can be put back as it was on an earlier date.
  • Contact the Hub administrator first. The Technical Guide is written so that another volunteer can step in if needed.
